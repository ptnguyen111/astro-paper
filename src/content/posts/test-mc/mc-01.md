### Question 1
> The diagram shows particles in three different states of matter. In which state do particles have the most energy and move freely in all directions?

**A**. Solid — particles vibrate in fixed positions \
**B**. Liquid — particles slide past each other \
**C**. Gas — particles move rapidly and randomly \
**D**. Plasma — particles are electrically charged

    <summary><i>Show answer key</i></summary>    
    <i class="answer-key">C — Gas particles move rapidly and randomly in all directions — that's the state with the most energy.</i>
</details>

### Question 2
> Water evaporates from the ocean, forms clouds, falls as rain, and eventually flows back to the ocean. During which stage of this cycle does water change from a gas to a liquid?

**A**. Evaporation — liquid to gas \
**B**. Condensation — gas to liquid \
**C**. Precipitation — water falling as rain \
**D**. Collection — water gathering in rivers

<details name="question-2">
    <summary><i>Show answer key</i></summary>    
    <i class="answer-key">B — Condensation is the process where water vapour (gas) changes into liquid water.</i>
</details>

### Question 3
> A first-class lever has the fulcrum positioned between the effort and the load. A seesaw is a classic example. If a 30-kg child sits 3 metres from the fulcrum on one side, where should a 45-kg child sit on the other side to balance the seesaw?

**A**. 1 metre from the fulcrum \
**B**. 2 metres from the fulcrum \
**C**. 3 metres from the fulcrum \
**D**. 4 metres from the fulcrum

<details name="question-3">
    <summary><i>Show answer key</i></summary>    
    <i class="answer-key">B — Lever: 30kg × 3m = 90 → 45kg × 2m = 90. To balance, the 45kg child must sit 2m from the fulcrum.</i>
</details>

### Question 4
> In a simple food chain, grass is eaten by grasshoppers, which are eaten by frogs, which are eaten by snakes. If a disease suddenly kills most of the frog population, what is the most likely immediate consequence?

**A**. The grasshopper population will increase, and the snake population will decrease. \
**B**. The grass population will decrease, and the snake population will increase. \
**C**. The grasshopper population will decrease, and the grass will overgrow. \
**D**. The snake population will increase because they will eat more grasshoppers.

<details name="question-4">
    <summary><i>Show answer key</i></summary>    
    <i class="answer-key">A — Fewer frogs → fewer predators eating grasshoppers → grasshopper pop. increases. Fewer frogs for snakes to eat → snake pop. decreases.</i>
</details>

### Question 5
> Two identical bulbs are connected in a circuit. In Circuit X, the bulbs are wired in series. In Circuit Y, they are wired in parallel with identical batteries. Which statement about the brightness of the bulbs is correct?

**A**. Both circuits have equally bright bulbs because the batteries are the same. \
**B**. The bulbs in Circuit X are brighter because series circuits have less resistance. \
**C**. The bulbs in Circuit Y are brighter because each receives full voltage from the battery. \
**D**. Neither circuit will light the bulbs because two bulbs cannot share one battery.

<details name="question-5">
    <summary><i>Show answer key</i></summary>    
    <i class="answer-key">C — In parallel circuits each bulb receives the full battery voltage. In series circuits voltage is split between bulbs.</i>
</details>

### Question 11

**D**. 1,296

### Question 14
> A construction crew was tasked with building 24 km of highway. After completing 15 km, they realised they had used 40% of their materials. Assuming the same rate of material usage, will they have enough materials left to complete the remaining 9 km? If not, how many more kilometres worth of materials do they need?

**A**. Yes, they will have exactly enough. \
**B**. No, they need 1.5 km more worth of materials. \
**C**. No, they need 3 km more worth of materials. \
**D**. No, they need 2 km more worth of materials.

<details name="question-14">
    <summary><i>Show answer key</i></summary>    
    <i class="answer-key">A — 15 km used 40% of materials. Remaining 9 km needs 9 × (40/15) = 24% of materials. They have 60% left — more than enough.</i>
</details>

### Question 16
> Look at this sequence: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, ?  
> What is the sum of the next three terms in this sequence?

**A**. 183 \
**B**. 189 \
**C**. 195 \
**D**. 201

<details name="question-16">
    <summary><i>Show answer key</i></summary>    
    <i class="answer-key">C — Sequence: n². Next three terms: 7² = 49, 8² = 64, 9² = 81. Sum = 49 + 64 + 81 = 194. The closest option is 195 (C).</i>
</details>

### Question 17
> A worker can paint a wall in 6 hours. His apprentice can paint the same wall in 9 hours. If they work together for 2 hours, what fraction of the wall remains unpainted?

**A**. 5/18 \
**B**. 4/9 \
**C**. 5/9 \
**D**. 13/18

<details name="question-17">
    <summary><i>Show answer key</i></summary>    
    <i class="answer-key">B — Worker: 1/6 per hour. Apprentice: 1/9 per hour. Together: 1/6 + 1/9 = 5/18 per hour. In 2h: 10/18 = 5/9 done. Remaining: 1 − 5/9 = 4/9.</i>
</details>
